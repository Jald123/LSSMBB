⏱️ Mastering Time & Flow MBB Standard

A Project Charter defines "Where we are going," but the Gantt Chart defines "How long the journey takes." This tool identifies the Critical Path—the specific sequence of tasks that, if delayed by 1 day, delays the entire project by 1 day.

Origin: Validated by Henry Gantt (1917) for WWI logistics. Before this, factories ran on "gut feel." It revolutionized the ability to predict delivery dates.

The Power of Visualization:
  • Dependency Logic: Shows that you cannot "Pilot" (Phase 4) until you "Measure" (Phase 2).
  • Resource Conflicts: Reveals when one person is assigned to 3 tasks at once.
  • Reality Check: Most projects underestimate time by 50%. This tool forces you to map every day.

Critical Path Management

🔎 THE "CRITICAL PATH"

Focus 80% of your energy on the "Critical Path" tasks. If a non-critical task slips by a week, it doesn't matter (Slack Time). If a Critical Task slips by an hour, you are late.

⚠️ COMMON TRAPS
  • Analysis Paralysis: Listing every single email as a task. Keep tasks to >1 day duration.
  • Padding: Adding "buffer" to every task. Instead, add one "Project Buffer" at the end.
  • Ignoring Weekends: This tool assumes working days. Remember that 5 days duration = 1 calendar week.
🔗 VISUAL DEPENDENCY TYPES
1. Finish-to-Start (FS)

"Task B starts after Task A finishes." (Standard)

2. Start-to-Start (SS)

"Task B starts when Task A starts." (Parallel work)

3. Finish-to-Finish (FF)

"Task A and B must finish at the same time."

🌍 REAL-WORLD SCENARIOS
🏥 Hospital (Hospital Wing)
SS Dependency: "Staff Training" starts while "Equipment Installation" is still ongoing.
🏭 Manufacturing (New Line)
FS Dependency: "Pour Concrete" must finish 7 days before "Install Robots". (Critical Path risk)

⚖️ The Path of Life or Death

This is the Critical Path. Great managers don't monitor every task equally—they focus 90% of their energy on the tasks with Zero Slack ×

⚖️ Zero Slack: The Medical "Critical Path"

In an emergency surgery scheduled for 60 minutes from now:

Sterilizing Instruments (60m): Zero Slack. Any 1-minute delay means a 1-minute delay in surgery. Critical Task.

Checking Insurance (15m): 45m Slack. The surgery still starts on time even if this is delayed. Flexible Task.

Key Takeaway: Zero Slack tasks have no "buffer." If they slip, the patient care slips. .

Master the art of Lead Time ×

⏱️ Lead Time: The "Head Start"

Example: Preparing an OR.
You don't wait for anesthesia (Task A) to be 100% finished before setting up instruments (Task B). You overlap them.

Definition: Starting a second task before the first is finished. This "compresses" the timeline. (overlapping tasks to compress timelines) and respect the science of Lag Time ×

⏳ Lag Time: The "Waiting" Period

Example: Plaster Cast.
After applying the cast (Task A), you must wait 24 hours for it to harden before Task B (Walking).

Definition: A mandatory delay between tasks. You aren't "working," but time must pass. (mandatory physical or biological waiting periods). Timing isn't just a number; it's a strategic resource.

🌪️ Navigating the Fog of Initiation

Early precision is a looming disaster. The Cone of Uncertainty ×

🌪️ Cone of Uncertainty: Project Estimation

Example: Patient's Length of Stay.
- Admission: Range is broad (2–10 days).
- Diagnosis: Range narrows (3–5 days).
- After Surgery: Accuracy peaks (4 days).

Definition: Knowledge increases over time, shrinking the "width" of uncertainty from guess to fact. teaches us to use ranges early and narrow our focus only as knowledge is gained. Respect the precision paradox.
