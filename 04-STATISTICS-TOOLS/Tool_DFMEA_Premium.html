🛡️ The Shield of Risk

"Anything that can go wrong, will go wrong." – Murphy's Law.
The FMEA is not just a form; it is a prediction engine. Apollo 13 didn't have an FMEA line item for "Oxygen Tank Explosion." You should.

Origin (1949): Developed by the US Military (Procedures for Performing Failure Mode Analysis). Adpoted deeply by NASA (Apollo Program) to prevent catastrophic failure in space. Ford brought it to the auto industry in the 1980s.

The Power:
  • Prevention over Inspection: Most tools inspect quality out. FMEA designs quality in by predicting failure before it happens.
  • RPN Logic: It forces you to balance Severity (S), Occurrence (O), and Detection (D). It proves that a "rare" but "deadly" defect is still a priority.

How to lead a Risk Assessment Analysis

1. SEVERITY = 10 (SAFETY)

If Severity is a 9 or 10 (Safety/Regulatory issue), you MUST take action to mitigate it, regardless of the RPN. Even if it happens once in a million years (Low Occurrence), you cannot accept a safety risk.

2. THE WEAKNESS OF "DETECTION"

Improving Detection (inspecting more) is the weakest form of mitigation. It relies on humans or sensors not failing. The goal of a Black Belt is to lower Occurrence (prevent the defect from being made) or lower Severity (design it so failure isn't fatal).

3. PARETO YOUR RISKS

Don't use an arbitrary RPN cutoff (like "Anything over 100"). Use a Pareto approach. Fix the top 20% of your risk items first. Then re-evaluate.
