POKA-YOKE STRATEGY

History of Origin

Coined by Shigeo Shingo in the 1960s at Toyota. Originally called "Baka-Yoke" (idiot-proofing), it was renamed to Poka-Yoke (mistake-proofing) to protect employee dignity while maintaining system rigor. It focuses on processes, not people.

WHAT IS IT?

Poka-Yoke is a Japanese term that means "mistake-proofing" or "error-proofing". It involves designing a process or tool so that it's impossible to make a mistake.

WHY USE IT?

Human vigilance is unreliable. Poka-Yoke ensures zero defects by removing the human factor from the equation of process quality.

WHEN TO ADD?

Essential during the Improve phase to "lock in" standard work and prevent the process from backsliding into old habits.
