Process Capability AI ENABLED

Measure Perfection. Reduce Defects. Are you meeting customer limits?

🔍 What Is It?
Measures how well your process output falls within specification limits (LSL, USL).
⏱️ When To Use?
After stabilizing a process (Control Charts) to prove it meets customer needs.
💡 Why Use It?
High Cpk (>1.33) means virtually zero defects. Low Cpk means unhappy customers.
📋 Requirements
Normal Data + Stable Process + Specs (LSL/USL).
📐 Standard Equation
Cp = (USL - LSL) / 6σ
Cpk = Min[ (USL-x̄)/3σ, (x̄-LSL)/3σ ]
🧠 Interpretation (Click for charts)
Cpk < 1: BAD (Defects).
Cpk > 1.33: GOOD (Capable).
Cp > Cpk: Process is Off-Center.
🎯 Reference Values
Six Sigma Level = 6.0 (Cpk=2.0).
3 Sigma Level = 3.0 (Cpk=1.0).
🚀 Phase Relevance
Measure: Baseline capability.
Improve: Verify solution impact.

📊 Understanding This Chart

Blue Curve: Your process distribution (bell curve). Shows how your data is spread.

Red Lines (LSL/USL): Customer specification limits. Data beyond these = DEFECTS.

Green Dashed Line: Process Mean. Ideally centered between limits.

White Dots: Your actual data points.

Key Insight: If the curve extends past the red lines, you're producing defects. A narrow, centered curve = Capable process.

🚗 The Garage Analogy

Imagine your Data is a Car and the Limits are a Garage.

1. Cp = "Can it fit?"

Cp just checks if the car is small enough for the garage. It doesn't care if you park badly.

2. Cpk = "Did you park safely?"

Cpk checks if you actually parked in the middle. If you scrape the wall, that's a defect!

High Cpk (Good Parking)
Small Car, Centered. Safe!
High Cp, Low Cpk (Bad Driver)
Small Car, but parked on the wall.
Low Cp (Car Too Big)
Car simply doesn't fit. You need a bigger garage!

"The goal is a small car (Low Variation) parked perfectly in the middle (Centered Process)."

ARE YOU CAPABLE? 🤔

Two ways to understand Process Capability (Cp/Cpk)

The Scenario: Parking your Car Your Process is driving the car. The Specification Limits (LSL/USL) are the walls of your garage.
📐

1. Cp = "Can it Fit?"

Cp compares the Width of the Car vs the Width of the Garage.

It assumes you are a Perfect Driver. It doesn't care if you actually crash, just if it's theoretically possible to fit.

💥

2. Cpk = "Did you Park?"

Cpk looks at Where you actually parked.

If Cpk is low, you scraped the paint (produced a Defect), even if the car was small enough to fit!

The Scenario: ER Wait Times The Goal: See doctor within 30 mins (USL). The Process: Patient Arrival to Doc.
🏥

1. Cp = Potential

"Do we have enough doctors/rooms to see everyone in 30 mins?"

If Cp > 1: Yes, our capacity is good enough on paper.

📉

2. Cpk = Reality

"Are patients ACTUALLY waiting less than 30 mins?"

If Cp is High but Cpk is Low, it means we have the rooms, but our triage process is inefficient (Off-Centered).
