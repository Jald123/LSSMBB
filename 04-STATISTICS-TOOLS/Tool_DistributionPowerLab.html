Always check Normality before running a T-Test. Use Power to justify budget.

🔋 Distribution & Power Guide

Use this lab to master the behavior of data and test sensitivity.

📊 Probability Visualizer

Normal (Gaussian): The "Bell Curve". Most continuous data (height, time, weight) fits here.
Binomial: Pass/Fail outcomes (e.g., Coin flips, Defective Parts).
Poisson: Rare events over time (e.g., Accidents per year).

How to Execute:

  1. Select Type: Determine if your data is Continuous (Normal) or Discrete (Binomial/Poisson).
  2. Adjust Parameters: Use sliders to see how shifting Mean (Center) or Sigma (Spread) affects the shape.
  3. Check Tails: Notice that as Sigma increases, the "tails" get fatter (more defects).
⚠️ Important Consideration: Never assume data is Normal! Use the Anderson-Darling Test (in Stats Calculator) to verify before running T-Tests.

🔋 Sample Size & Power

What: Power (1 - Beta) is the probability of finding a difference IF one exists.

How to Execute:

  1. Set Alpha: Usually 0.05 (5% risk of False Positive).
  2. Set Power: Target 0.80 (80%) or 0.90 (90%).
  3. Define Delta: What is the *smallest* shift you care about? (e.g., 0.5 sec improvement).
  4. Input Sigma: What is your historical Standard Deviation?
⚠️ Important Consideration: If you try to detect a TINY Delta with HIGH Noise (Sigma), the calculator will tell you to collect thousands of samples. This is the "Cost of Precision".

Real-World Scenarios

🏥 Clinical Trial (Power)
Scenario: Testing a new drug.
Risk: If n is too small, we might say "Drug doesn't work" when it actually DOES.
Action: Calc Sample Size to ensure 90% Power.
🏭 Manufacturing (Poisson)
Scenario: Painting car doors.
Data: Counting "specks of dust" per door.
Model: Use Poisson Distribution to predict defect rates.
