Quality Function Deployment (QFD)

📜 History & Origin

Developed by Yoji Akao in Japan (1966). It was first successfully deployed at Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (1972) for tanker design, but became famous when Toyota adopted it in the late 70s, reducing pre-production costs by 60%. It was brought to the US by Xerox and Ford in the 1980s.

🤔 What is it?

A structured method to translate Customer Requirements (Voice of Customer) into appropriate Technical Requirements (Voice of Engineer). The "House of Quality" is the primary visual tool used in this process.

💡 Why & When to Use

  • Define Phase (DMAIC): To ensure the project scope aligns with actual customer needs.
  • Design Phase (DMADV): Critical for designing new products/services to ensure "Quality by Design".
  • Why? To prevent "Gold Plating" (adding features customers don't want) and ensure all engineering efforts directly impact customer satisfaction.

🛠️ How to Build (The Steps)

1. VOC (What) Capture customer wants. Rate Importance 1-5.
2. Specs (How) Define measurable engineering metrics.
3. Matrix Map strong (9), medium (3), or weak (1) correlations.
4. Roof Identify design trade-offs/conflicts (+/-).
5. Competition Benchmark us vs. them on a 1-5 scale.
6. Analysis Calculate Absolute Weights to find CTQs.

HOUSE OF QUALITY STRATEGY

The Ultimate Bridge between Customer Needs and Technical Reality.

History of QFD

Quality Function Deployment (QFD) was developed by Yoji Akao in 1966. It was designed to ensure that quality is built into the product from the design phase, rather than just inspected at the end. It ensures "The Voice of the Customer" is never lost in translation.

WHAT IS IT?

A structured methodology for translating customer requirements (WHATs) into specific technical specifications (HOWs).

WHY USE IT?

It prevents scope creep, reduces development time, and ensures the highest customer satisfaction by focusing on critical drivers.

THE ROOF?

The "Roof" of the House identifies Engineering Trade-offs. It shows how improving one spec might negatively impact another.
