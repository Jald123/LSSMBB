LEAN

Focus on Speed & Efficiency

  • Eliminates WASTE (Muda) from processes
  • Streamlines workflows & reduces wait times
  • Optimizes resource utilization
  • Focuses on "Flow" and "Pull"
  • Goal: Faster Care Delivery
🎯

SIX SIGMA

Focus on Quality & Consistency

  • Reduces VARIATION in processes
  • Data-driven decision making (DMAIC)
  • Minimizes defects and medical errors
  • Improves process capability (Cp/Cpk)
  • Goal: Better Outcomes & Safety

The Evolution of Excellence

Early 1900s: The Foundation
Henry Ford & Frederick Taylor

Ford's Assembly Line reduced production time from 12 hours to 93 minutes. Taylor introduced scientific management and improved efficiency.

1940s-80s: The Birth of Lean
Toyota Production System (TPS)

Taiichi Ohno developed JIT (Just-In-Time) and defined the 7 Wastes to solve lack of resources in post-war Japan. The term "Lean" emerges in the 80s via MIT.

1980s: Six Sigma Emerges
Motorola & Bill Smith

Motorola aimed to reduce defect rates in electronics. Coined "Six Sigma" (3.4 defects per million).

1990s: The Powerhouse
General Electric (GE)

Jack Welch made Six Sigma a strategic priority, proving it could save billions. It became the global gold standard for business.

2000s - Present: Healthcare Integration
Cleveland Clinic, Mayo, John Hopkins

Hospitals began adopting LSS to improve patient safety (reducing medical errors) and efficiency (discharge times). Today, it drives Value-Based Care.

Defining "Value" in Healthcare

In Lean, value is defined ONLY by the customer (The Patient).

👍 Value-Added (VA)

  • Directly improves patient health/outcomes
  • The patient "cares" about this step
  • Standard of care procedures
  • Examples: Diagnosis, Treatment, Nursing Care, Surgery.

👎 Non-Value-Added (NVA)

  • Consumes resources but adds no health value
  • Patient would not pay for it if they had a choice
  • Can be "Necessary" (Regulatory) or "Waste" (Pure muda)
  • Examples: Waiting, Redundant Paperwork, Searching for supplies, Re-testing.

The 3 Key Questions to Identify Flow:

Does it change the outcome?
Is it done right the first time?
Does the patient care?
