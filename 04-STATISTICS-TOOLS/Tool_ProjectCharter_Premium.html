CRITICAL ALERT: SCOPE CREEP

×

The Clinical Impact: Why It Risks Patient Care

In healthcare, Scope Creep is like a virus that infects your project timeline. What starts as a simple mission—like "Reducing Patient Discharge Time"—can quickly mutate into "Redesigning Pharmacy Workflows" or "Training Nursing Staff on New Tablets."

The Result? Resources are bleed dry, frontline staff face burnout from "change fatigue," and the original goal—improving patient flow—is never achieved. In Six Sigma, if you try to fix everything at once, you fix nothing.

Simplified Rule

If it's not in the original Project Charter, it doesn't exist. Lock the door to "While we're at it..." requests.

Master Tip

Use the Out-of-Scope section of this tool as your shield. Defend it fiercely to hit your financial targets.

The DMAIC Charter Philosophy

A Project Charter is not just a document; it is a governance contract. It establishes the authority to occupy resources and the commitment to hit financial targets.

1. Project Title

MASTER ARCHITECT CHALLENGE: FIX THE TITLE

Target Case: "Fix the Hospital"
"Hospital Performance Project"
"Reduce Bed Turnover Time in ER Ward"

The Story of the Ghost Project

A study by LSS Quality Quarterly (2023) warns that 70% of projects fail because they lack a specific "Object" of improvement. Without it, the project haunts the organization without ever being finished.

70%Fail due to unclear titles [LSS Quarterly]
40%Abandoned due to poor alignment [MBB Insights]
🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"[Verb] + [Improvement Object] + [Specific Process/Location]"

Poor Content Critique

"Fix the processing VAGUE" Nouns Matters 'Processing' is too broad. Is it an invoice? A patient? A machine cycle?
"Install new CRM software SOLUTION" No Gap This is a solution, not a title. The title should describe the improvement of a metric.

2. Business Case

MASTER ARCHITECT CHALLENGE: THE WHY

Scenario: The CEO asks: "Why should I fund this instead of the new marketing campaign?"
"Because the team is really frustrated with the process."
"It directly recovers $200k in lost margin, funding 50% of our growth goal."

The $45k Invisible Leak

A tech firm was losing $45k monthly due to server downtime. Leadership ignored it until the Belt linked this to 3% of total revenue. **The Lesson?** Numbers turn an 'annoyance' into a 'strategic priority.' [Ref: LSS World Journal, 2023]

85%Approval rate with clear ROI [LSS World]
$45kAvg monthly loss of "Ignored" problems

🛡️ STRATEGIC MORALE

"A business case is not just about the numbers; it's about the will to survive. In Lean Six Sigma, we don't fix processes for the sake of efficiency—we fix them to protect our people's focus and our organization's future. If the case doesn't bleed red ink, the management won't see green light."

MBB WISDOM #42

Poor Content Critique

"The moral is low NOT FINANCIAL" Weak Case Morale is important, but executives need to see how low morale impacts the balance sheet ($).

3. Problem Statement

MASTER ARCHITECT CHALLENGE: FIND THE GAP

Check: Which of these is a VALID Problem Statement?
"We need to install a new CRM to track leads better."
"Since Jan, Ward 4 has a 25% error rate in discharge papers, delaying beds by 4 hours."

The Vague Symptom Trap

A plant manager once said, "Our output is too slow." The team worked for months and failed. Why? Because "too slow" is a symptom. When they redefined it as "20% cycle time increase in Line B since Q2," the root cause became obvious. [Ref: Six Sigma Daily, 2024]

50%Projects solved by proper definition
3xFaster solving with specific data [HS Digest]
🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"What (Object), Where (Process), When (Date), and Extent (Magnitude)."

Poor Content Critique

"Our processing is too slow OPINION" Subjective 'Too slow' means different things to different people. Use exact time or percentages.

4. SMART Goal

Define Success

MASTER ARCHITECT CHALLENGE: DEFINE SUCCESS

"Reduce scrap as much as possible by Q4."
"Reduce scrap from 8.5% to 2.0% by Dec 31st."

The Finish Line Hallucination

Projects with defined deadlines and baseline targets are 60% more likely to succeed. Without a "By When," the project is just an eternal hobby. Numbers create the commitment required for change. [Ref: PMI Pulse of the Profession, 2023]

20%Performance boost with SMART goals [PMI]
80%Delay reduction vs undefined goals [LSS Today]
🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"To [Improve/Reduce] [Metric] from [Baseline] to [Target] by [Specific Deadline]."

MBB Best Practice

"Reduce Lead Time from 12 to 5 days by Dec 31."
"Decrease Scrap from 8.2% to 2.0% within 6 months."

Poor Content Critique

"Improve quality as much as possible NOT MEASURABLE" No Benchmark 'As much as possible' cannot be verified. Use a hard number.

5. Scope Control

MASTER ARCHITECT CHALLENGE: GUARD THE DOORS

"Sure, we can include the packaging line too."
"Packaging line is Out-of-Scope. We will log it for Phase 2."

The Virus of 'While We're At It'

55% of project overruns are caused by small, "helpful" additions to the scope. In Six Sigma, if it's not in the original Charter, it doesn't exist. Defend your boundaries fiercely to hit your financial targets. [Ref: Harvard Business Review, 2022]

55%Overruns caused by Scope Creep [HBR]
40%Faster delivery with rigid scope [Lean Inst.]

Poor Content Critique

"Include all company departments TOO BROAD" Boiling Ocean Trying to fix everything at once ensures you fix nothing. Stick to one process.

6. Governance

MASTER ARCHITECT CHALLENGE: CHOOSE CHAMPION

"I need a high-level Sponsor."
"I need a dedicated Process Owner."

Sarah's Ghost Controls

Sarah fixed a hospital process perfectly. It worked for two weeks. But with no Process Owner to catch the baton, the gains disappeared in a month. 90% of sustainable success depends on who owns the process after the Belt leaves. [Ref: ASQ Quality Progress, 2021]

90%Failure rate without a Process Owner [ASQ]
73%Success boost with active Sponsors [Prosci]

Poor Content Critique

"Sponsor: The CEO TOO DISTANT" Authority Gap The CEO is too far from the daily process. You need the person who has the budget and pain for THIS specific area.
STAKEHOLDER INFLUENCE MATRIX ARCHITECT VIEW

7. COPQ & Savings

MASTER ARCHITECT CHALLENGE: THE MATH

"Monthly leak: $5,000"
"Monthly leak: $50,000"

The $50,000 Monthly Mistake

Many companies lose 20-30% of their revenue to the "Hidden Factory"—rework, scrap, and lost time that nobody measures. By quantifying the Cost of Poor Quality (COPQ), you turn invisible waste into a bankable ROI. [Ref: Quality Digest, 2023]

20%Avg revenue lost to COPQ (Service) [Qual. Digest]
40%Avg revenue lost to COPQ (Mfg) [LSS Inst.]

Poor Content Critique

"Savings: Improved efficiency NOT QUANTIFIED" Vague ROI Efficiency doesn't pay the bills. How many hours or dollars are saved? Use the calculator below.

8. Milestones

MASTER ARCHITECT CHALLENGE: CHRONOLOGY

"Kickoff -> Implementation -> Analyze -> Control"
"Charter -> Baseline -> Root Cause -> Pilot"

The DMAIC Velocity Tempo

Missing the very first milestone (Define Phase) increases the risk of project cancellation by 65%. In Six Sigma, fast starts often lead to long finishes. Respect the gate reviews to maintain organizational momentum. [Ref: iSixSigma Research, 2023]

65%Cancellation risk if 1st date missed [iSixSigma]
100%Correlation: Gate Reviews & Velocity [ASQ]

Poor Content Critique

"Timeline: To be determined NO URGENCY" Open Ended A project with no dates is a hobby. Set estimated phase gates now, even if they shift later.
