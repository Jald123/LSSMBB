The DMAIC Charter Philosophy

A Project Charter is not just a document; it is a governance contract between the project lead and executive sponsors. It establishes the authority to occupy resources and the commitment to hit specified financial targets.

🥋 Master Black Belt Hint:

"A project without a charter is a hobby. A project with a bad charter is a liability. Focus on the 'Problem Statement' first—it is the heart that drives the entire DMAIC machine."

1. Project Title

What & Why?

A concise label describing the focus. It serves as the "Elevator Pitch" for your project in leadership portfolios.

🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"[Verb] + [Improvement Object] + [Specific Process/Location]"

CLARITY + CORE METRIC + SCOPE = PERFECT TITLE

Master Black Belt Best Practice

"Reduce Lead Time in Hiring Process"
"Optimize Fuel Consumption in Fleet B"
"Decrease Bed Turnover Lag (Ward 4)"
"Eliminate Billing Errors in AP Dept"
"Improve Order Pick Accuracy (Warehouse X)"

Poor Content Critique

"Fix the Hospital" Vague Too broad. An MBB cannot tell if this is about surgery, floors, or finance.
"Install SAP Software" Solutioneering This is a task, not a problem. The title should focus on the *gap* the software fixes.
"Project Quality" No Verb Lacks directional intent (Reduce/Increase). It's a category, not a project title.

2. Business Case

What & Why?

The strategic justification. Why is this project more important than 10 others? Focus on Money, Risk, or Market survival.

🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"Currently [Problem] is costing $[Amount]. If not solved, we risk [Loss of Customers/Market/Compliance]."

STRATEGIC PAIN + FINANCIAL LEAK + RISK = COMPELLING CASE

MBB Best Practice

"Currently losing $1.2M annually due to inventory spoilage."
"Missed delivery targets are causing 12% customer churn."
"New regulatory compliance requires < 1% error by Q4."
"Reducing downtime will unlock $500k in hidden capacity."
"Strategic goal to be market leader in speed; we are 40% slower than competition."

🛡️ STRATEGIC MORALE

"A business case is not just about the numbers; it's about the will to survive. In Lean Six Sigma, we don't fix processes for the sake of efficiency—we fix them to protect our people's focus and our organization's future. If the case doesn't bleed red ink, the management won't see green light."

MBB WISDOM #42

Poor Content Critique

"Everyone thinks we need to do this." Emotional Lacks data. An executive cannot fund 'feelings'. Mention the financial impact or missed KPIs.
"This is a great chance to learn Six Sigma." Self-Serving A business case is for the *business*, not your personal training. Focus on organizational ROI.
"Better quality will result in good things." Vague What 'good things'? Be specific (e.g., lower warranty costs, higher NPS).

3. Problem Statement

What & Why?

The core of the charter. Must define What, Where, When, and Extent. Never include a cause or solution.

🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"Since [Date], [Process] at [Location] is [Problem Metric] resulting in [Direct Impact]."

4 W'S (WHAT, WHERE, WHEN, WHY) + BASELINE DATA = HIGH IQ PROB STMT

MBB Best Practice

"In 2025, Line A reports 8% scrap rate vs 2% goal, costing $50k/mo."
"Current Discharge lag in Ward 4 is 4.2 hrs (Target < 2 hrs)."
"Since Q1, 15% of invoices contain price errors, causing rework."
"Technical support FCR is 45%, benchmark is 80%."
"Employee turnover in Sales is 22% (Last 12 months)."

Poor Content Critique

"Scrap is high because the CAUSE machine is old." Bias You've already diagnosed the problem. DMAIC's goal is to *prove* the cause, not guess it in 'Define'.
"Discharge process SOLUTION needs new software." Solutioneering You are pushing a solution. Focus only on the 'Gap' or 'Pain' right now.
"Our processing is NO DATA too slow." Opinion 'Too slow' is subjective. Use seconds, minutes, or percentages.

4. SMART Goal Statement

What & Why?

The target state. It must be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. It is your yardstick of success.

🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"To [Improve/Reduce] [Metric] from [Baseline] to [Target] by [Specific Deadline]."

BASELINE TARGET + DEADLINE + MEASURABLE METRIC = SUCCESS

MBB Best Practice

"Reduce Lead Time from 12 to 5 days by Dec 31."
"Decrease Scrap from 8.2% to 2.0% within 6 months."
"Increase FCR from 45% to 75% for Q3 results."
"Lower Bed Turnaround to < 2 hrs by Aug."
"Reduce Price Errors to < 0.5% by Year End."

Poor Content Critique

"Improve quality VAGUE as much as possible." No Measure 'As much as possible' cannot be benchmarked. Use a number.
"Fix the error rate NO TIME." No Deadline Without a 'By When', projects linger forever without urgency.
"Achieve zero errors UNACHIEVABLE in 2 days." Timeline DMAIC takes weeks/months. 'Zero' is rarely achievable in a single sprint.

5. Scope Control

What & Why?

Defines the boundaries. Protects the project from "Scope Creep"—the slow expansion of tasks that kills timelines.

🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"Focus on [Process A], [Location B], [Shift C]. Excludes [Process D]."

CLEAR BOUNDARIES + STAKEHOLDER AGREEMENT = NO CREEP

MBB Best Practice

In: Dept A only. Out: All shared services.
In: Manual Data Entry. Out: System automation.
In: Day Shift. Out: Night/Weekend Shifts.
In: Triage to Bed. Out: Discharge process.
In: Product X. Out: Product Y and Z.

Poor Content Critique

"Fix ALL hospital operations." Ocean Boiling Too big to succeed. An MBB would reject this to prevent project failure.
"In-Scope: Everything." No Shield 'Everything' means you can't say 'No' to any request. You'll never finish.
"N/A MISSING" Dangerous Leaving the boundary undefined is an open invitation for scope creep.

6. Governance

What & Why?

Roles define authority. A project without a real Sponsor is just a suggestion.

🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"SPONSOR = Budget Authority. OWNER = Process Receiver. BELT = Expert Diver."

RIGHT PEOPLE + RIGHT ROLES + EXECUTIVE SPONSOR = GOVERNANCE

MBB Best Practice

Sponsor: VP Operations. Authority to sign budget.
Process Owner: Warehouse Mgr. Owns the results.
Black Belt: Senior Analyst. Expert lead.
Finance: AP Director. Validates savings.
HR: Benefits Lead. In-scope SME.

Poor Content Critique

Sponsor: NAME ONLY John Doe Low Power If John Doe has no budget authority over the process, the project will stall.
Owner: Same as Lead. Conflict The Belt Lead can't 'own' the process they are fixing; results must be handed back to a line manager.
Team: Everyone in the dept. Unmanageable Too many stakeholders. Keep core teams to 5-7 dedicated people.

7. COPQ & Savings

What & Why?

The financial justification. Must show the "Hard Dollars" to be saved. CFOs focus here.

🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"COPQ = [Defect Rate] x [Unit Cost] x [Volume]. GOAL = Hard $ Impact."

REDUCTION IN WASTE x UNIT COST = HARD PROFIT

MBB Best Practice

Material scrap cost: $250k / yr.
Rework labor: 40 hrs/wk @ $50/hr.
Overtime savings: $120k / yr target.
Capacity gain: Add $400k in revenue capability.
Inventory carry cost: 15% reduction goal.

Poor Content Critique

"Savings will be HUGE." Non-Quantitative Use numbers. "Huge" is not a line item on a balance sheet.
"Saves TIME for everyone." Soft Only Unless you decrease headcount or increase billable output, time alone isn't 'Hard Savings'.
No baseline used. Baseless You can't calculate a reduction if you don't know the current loss value.

8. Milestones

What & Why?

The 5 DMAIC Phase dates. It creates accountability and prevents "Project Fatigue".

🏆 MBB GOLDEN STANDARD:

"Define (Wk 2), Measure (Wk 6), Analyze (Wk 9), Improve (Wk 14), Control (Wk 18)."

FIXED DEADLINES + GATE REVIEWS = VELOCITY

MBB Best Practice

Define Gate: Week 2 Close.
Measure Gate: Week 5 Close.
Analyze Gate: Week 9 Close.
Improve Gate: Week 13 Close.
Control Gate: Week 16 Close.

Poor Content Critique

"All phases SAME DATE in Dec." Impossible DMAIC is sequential. You cannot finish 'Measure' and 'Improve' on the same day.
Total project time: TOO LONG 2 Years. Fatigue Six Sigma projects should ideally land between 3-6 months. 2 years is a capital build, not DMAIC.
"TBD" for all dates. No Commitment Leadership won't sign a charter with an open-ended timeline.
